\title{EMBER: Emergent Memory-Based Encoding and Reactivation, a bioinspired memory for autonomous robots under the e-MDB cognitive architecture}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous --- double-blind review}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A robot learning over its service life must decide what to keep in
fixed-size memory. The episodic buffer of e-MDB, a cognitive architecture for
lifelong open-ended robot learning, keeps the latest episodes whatever
their value. We present EMBER (Emergent Memory-Based Encoding and
Reactivation), which casts such a memory as a six-axis design space ---read, write,
surprise weighting, decay, eviction and reinforcement--- and evaluates all
\result{exp03_axis_liveness:n_genotipos|int}{576} configurations of one engine,
among them a nearest-neighbour proxy of the e-MDB buffer, on how many
importance-tagged experiences survive a stream overrunning capacity. The
memory never sees the tags, only a surprise signal. The best configuration
differs from that proxy on two axes, surprise-weighted encoding and
weakest-trace eviction, and from the proxy neither changes anything alone. On a
separable surprise signal it retains
\result{exp01_nas_full:frontier.scores.rare_retention|f3}{0.983} of tagged
experiences against \result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.scores.rare_retention|f3}{0.050}.
It keeps the top rank in 13 of 14 held-out stream families; when recurring
experience fits in memory it retains
\result{exp08_imperfect_salience:settings.main.conditions.auc500.policies.frontier.recall.mean|f2}{0.60}
against
\result{exp08_imperfect_salience:settings.main.conditions.auc500.policies.fifo.recall.mean|f2}{0.03}
at chance-level surprise; no external replacement rule beats it. In
MiniGrid, with surprise from reward prediction, it retains
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:exp06_ampliado.aleatoria_recompensa.resultados.frontera.tasa|pct}{47.2}\,\%
of rewarded events against
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:exp06_ampliado.aleatoria_recompensa.resultados.FIFO.tasa|pct}{0.7}\,\%.
We hypothesize that which mechanism matters depends on the traces recurring
experience occupies after consolidation relative to capacity: at ratios $\le 0.5$
the write axis explains
\result{exp02_threshold_grid:regimenes.compression.eta2_write|pct}{73.0}\,\%
of the variance, above one
\result{exp02_threshold_grid:regimenes.selection.eta2_write|pct}{1.6}\,\%.
Merging needs repeated encounters separable from distinct ones, which
embeddings of natural images (CIFAR-100) are not. Better retention is not yet
better behaviour: an episodic controller on the frontier integrates less return
than on FIFO.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A cognitive robot that learns over its lifetime must decide what is worth
keeping, keep it accessible in proportion to its relevance, and retrieve it from
a partial or degraded cue. These requirements map onto three well-studied
properties of biological memory: selective encoding modulated by surprise,
use-regulated consolidation and decay, and content-addressable retrieval that
is robust to noise.

The episodic buffer of e-MDB (\emph{epistemic Multilevel Darwinist Brain})
\cite{romero2022thesis,romero2023ica} ---in its public ROS2 implementation, a
fixed-capacity FIFO (first-in-first-out) queue \cite{emdbnodes}--- satisfies
none of the three. It keeps whatever was perceived most recently, reads
episodes back by position or as training batches, and discards the oldest when
full, whatever its importance. The bioinspired-memory literature offers
principled remedies: Sparse Distributed Memory (SDM) \cite{kanerva1988} for
noise-robust content-addressable retrieval; Hebbian engram consolidation
\cite{liu2012,hebb1949}; prediction-error gating of encoding, grounded in
dopaminergic modulation of hippocampal encoding \cite{lisman2005,shohamy2010}
and in the finding that larger reward-prediction errors improve episodic
memory \cite{rouhani2018}; and eviction by value rather than age, since memory
availability tracks the probability of future need \cite{anderson1991} and
forgetting is regulated in the service of decisions \cite{richards2017}. But
these mechanisms are usually evaluated in isolation, inside architectures built
to show them off, against baselines that do not share their design space, so a
reported gain may come from the protocol rather than the mechanism.

EMBER (Emergent Memory-Based Encoding and Reactivation) inverts that approach.
We define a discrete design space in which each mechanism is an axis, and
traverse it exhaustively. A \emph{configuration} is one choice on each of six
axes; all \result{exp03_axis_liveness:n_genotipos|int}{576} are enumerated, not
sampled. The e-MDB buffer ---more precisely, a FIFO-NN proxy of it that adds
nearest-neighbour retrieval--- is one point inside that space, which turns a
potentially circular comparison into a falsifiable question: where does this
\emph{incumbent} land, and which configuration is the \emph{frontier}, the
best-scoring point? Each configuration receives a \emph{surprise signal} with
every experience: \emph{novelty}, which the memory computes as one minus the
cosine similarity to its closest trace, or \emph{prediction error} in $[0,1]$
from a model of what was expected. It is scored on \emph{rare-event
retention}, the fraction of importance-tagged experiences still recoverable
after a stream many times longer than the capacity; the tag is used only for
scoring, never shown to the memory. We relate the results to
$r = K_{proto}/C$: the number of \emph{prototypes} (distinct recurring kinds of
experience) over the \emph{capacity}, the number of traces held before a write
forces an eviction. Thus $r<1$ means recurring experience fits with slots to
spare and $r>1$ that it does not; $K_{proto}$ is known on synthetic streams and
estimated on real ones.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item An exhaustive evaluation in which the e-MDB buffer is a point of the
  space. The frontier differs from the FIFO-NN proxy on two axes only,
  surprise-weighted encoding and weakest-trace eviction, and from the proxy
  neither changes anything alone. It outscores the proxy on a separable
  surprise signal, keeps that lead under degraded surprise and on held-out
  stream families, and is not beaten by seven memory-management rules from
  outside the space; the buffer's real sequential read scores lower still.
  \item A \emph{regime hypothesis}: which mechanism family governs retention is
  set by the ratio of the traces recurring experience occupies after
  consolidation to capacity, $r_{eff}$, which on clean streams equals $r$; the
  gating mechanism the literature most motivates has a negligible main effect
  but a large one conditional on the eviction rule that reads it.
  \item A two-phase benchmark of four memory substrates that admits them by
  reconstruction before testing retention under pressure: a pure spiking
  circuit fails on continuous vectors, and SDM's retention comes from gated
  eviction with exact erasure, not from superposition.
  \item Two preconditions that appear only on real data: consolidation needs
  repeated encounters separable from distinct ones, which embeddings of
  CIFAR-100 \cite{krizhevsky2009}, a 100-class natural-image benchmark, are not; and in MiniGrid \cite{minigrid}
  surprise must be aligned with what defines importance, which, when importance
  is reward, raises the frontier's retention from the floor to about half.
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} collects, in one place, what these results do
not show.

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Memory substrates.} EMBER compares four (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}):
SDM, the Engram Neural Network (ENN), a pure spiking circuit and a hybrid
Spiking-SDM. SDM \cite{kanerva1988} distributes each pattern
over many overlapping \emph{hard locations} with fixed random addresses, so a
noisy query still activates enough of the same ones to reconstruct the
original, and accuracy degrades gradually. Our instantiation draws 512 hard
locations as random unit vectors; a write adds $s\cdot k$, with $s$ the
encoding strength, to the counters of the 25 ($5\,\%$) most aligned with key
$k$, and a read sums the counters of the query's 25 nearest and normalizes.
Activation is by inner product rather than a Hamming radius, so the radius
adapts to local density (cf.\ \cite{kanerva2009}), and the memory keeps an
explicit trace list alongside the counters, returning the stored trace most
similar to the reconstruction. The \emph{engram}, the physical trace of a
memory \cite{semon1904,liu2012}, rests on long-term potentiation (LTP): neurons
that fire together strengthen their synapse \cite{hebb1949}. ENN is our
name ---not a prior architecture---
for the minimal online memory built on that rule: fast Hebbian weights updated
without backpropagation, where each write creates a trace or reinforces one
above a cosine threshold. Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP)
\cite{bipoo1998} applies the rule to individual spikes and groups neurons into
assemblies; the project's third proof of concept (PoC-3) verified on leaky
integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons that a partial cue recruits the rest of its
assembly.

\emph{Search, replay and memory management.} Neural architecture search
\cite{elsken2019} optimizes architectural decisions; memory-augmented networks
\cite{graves2014,munkhdalai2017} fix the memory type and learn to use it. We
instead make the memory mechanism the search variable over a fully enumerable
space, so no result can be attributed to an approximate optimizer.
Prioritized experience replay \cite{schaul2016} revisits transitions by
temporal-difference error, the principle behind prediction-error gating, and
Neural Episodic Control \cite{pritzel2017} reads a dictionary by similarity,
as SDM does; both fix those choices in advance. Outside biology, caches evict
by recency or frequency \cite{oneil1993,megiddo2003}; reservoir sampling keeps
a uniform sample of a stream \cite{vitter1985} and is a strong replay baseline
in continual learning \cite{chaudhry2019}; rehearsal methods select exemplars
by herding \cite{rebuffi2017}, gradient diversity \cite{aljundi2019}, or
surprise, reward and coverage in lifelong reinforcement learning
\cite{isele2018}, where replay mitigates forgetting \cite{rolnick2019}. These
methods optimize downstream learning under a fixed rule; EMBER measures
retention across a space of rules, and Section~\ref{sec:holds} evaluates seven
of them.

\emph{e-MDB and LOLA.} e-MDB descends from the Multilevel Darwinist Brain
\cite{bellas2010} and targets LOLA (\emph{Lifelong Open-ended Learning
Autonomy}) \cite{romero2023sensors}: a robot that acquires knowledge
continuously, in domains unknown at design time and without predefined tasks.
A long-term memory of \emph{cognitive nodes} ---perceptual classes, goals,
contexts, world and utility models, policies--- is activated from the current
perception \cite{duro2019} and coupled to a motivational engine of needs and
drives \cite{becerra2021,romero2022thesis,romero2023ica}. In the ROS2
implementation \cite{emdbnodes} each step yields an \texttt{Episode}, and the
\texttt{EpisodicBuffer} from which world and utility models are trained is a
bounded \texttt{deque}: episodes are appended, the oldest discarded, and read
by index or as a batch, with no importance criterion and no similarity search
(file \texttt{episodic\_buffer.py}; no publication describes it). In our space
its eviction and weighting are the configuration
\texttt{\{read: nn, write: append, strength: constant, decay: 1.0, evict: fifo,
reinforce: 0\}}; because that point retrieves by nearest neighbour, which the
real buffer cannot, we call it the \emph{FIFO-NN proxy} and treat it as an
optimistic stand-in. Fig.~\ref{fig:integration} shows where EMBER would enter.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:nas}

\subsection{The design space}

Each axis is a decision an engineer building an episodic memory has to make
anyway (Table~\ref{tab:axes}; Fig.~\ref{fig:write} shows how they compose into
one write). The \emph{read} axis returns the single closest
trace (\texttt{nn}), aggregates the three closest (\texttt{topk3}), or
superposes all traces within a similarity radius of the best match
(\texttt{radius}, the SDM read). The \emph{write} axis always creates a trace
(\texttt{append}) or first reinforces an existing one at cosine
$\geq 0.85$ (\texttt{merge}, Hebbian consolidation). The \emph{strength} axis,
the \emph{salience gate}, assigns the encoding weight $s$. \emph{Decay} erodes
unreinforced strength; \emph{evict} discards, when memory is full, the oldest
trace (\texttt{fifo}), the weakest (\texttt{min\_strength}), the least read
(\texttt{min\_utility}, LFU-like) or a random one; \emph{reinforce} raises a
trace's strength each time it is read. The
\result{exp03_axis_liveness:n_genotipos|int}{576} configurations are
parameterizations of one engine in which every mechanism is implemented once,
so any score difference is attributable to the axis settings alone. We reserve
\emph{architecture} for the four substrates of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark},
which differ in how a trace is physically represented.

\subsection{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Every detail is taken from the code that produced the results. Keys live in
$\mathbb{R}^{32}$ and are normalized at the memory boundary; every random draw
derives from an explicit seed, and within a seed all configurations receive the
identical stream.

\emph{Streams.} $K_{proto}$ centres are i.i.d.\ Gaussian vectors on the unit
sphere. A routine experience picks a prototype uniformly, adds Gaussian noise
($\sigma=0.05$ per dimension) and renormalizes; its payload is the prototype
identity. Rare experiences have fresh random keys and unique payloads, at
uniformly random positions. The robustness families of
Section~\ref{sec:regime} each move one knob of this generator: Zipf visit
frequencies, drift, intra-prototype noise, centre similarity, rare-event
prevalence, recurrence, and $C$ from 5 to 160. CIFAR-100 streams
(Section~\ref{sec:real}) use a different image of one of $K_{proto}$ classes
per visit and draw rare experiences from disjoint classes.

\emph{Surprise and importance.} Prediction error is drawn from
$\mathcal{N}(0.10,0.05)$ clipped to $[0,0.35]$ for routine experiences and
from $\mathcal{N}(0.90,0.05)$ clipped to $[0.65,1]$ for rare ones, so the
ranges do not overlap by construction. Novelty is one minus the highest cosine
similarity to a stored trace (1 when empty). The gate assigns $s=1$
(\texttt{constant}), $1+2\nu$ (\texttt{novelty}), $1+2e$ (\texttt{pred\_error})
or $1+2\nu+2e$ (\texttt{both}). The importance tag is the generator's rare
flag, used only for scoring.

\emph{Phase 1, reconstruction gate} (capacity never binding). R1: 10 patterns,
cues keeping 30/50/70\,\% of the dimensions, scored by the cosine of the
returned trace when its identity is correct and 0 otherwise. R2: 15 patterns
under Gaussian noise $\sigma\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}$, scored by identity
accuracy. R3: capacity 2, write $A$ then $B$, read $A$; 10 pairs, scored as R1.
R4: accuracy at $\sigma=0.15$ for loads $\{2,5,10,15,20,30\}$ with capacity
equal to load. The gate is the mean of R1--R4; admission requires $\geq 0.50$.

\emph{Phase 2, battery} ($C=20$). T1, rare-event retention: $K_{proto}=5$, 300
routine and 20 rare experiences; every 5 writes the memory is queried with an
already written key, so reinforcement can act; at the end each rare experience
is queried with its exact key and counts as retained if its payload returns at
similarity $\geq 0.85$. T2, noisy retrieval: 60 random keys with prediction
error $\sim\mathcal{U}(0.05,0.95)$ in 20 slots; 120 queries pick an item with
probability proportional to its prediction error and add noise $\sigma=0.30$.
T3, sequential interference: 4 blocks of 8 keys, each written 3 times,
prediction error 0.9 in the first block and 0.1 after; the score is the
fraction of first-block keys retained. The \emph{aggregate score} is the
unweighted mean of T1--T3, each averaged over seeds.

\emph{Seeds and statistics.} The search uses 3 seeds per configuration; the
threshold grid, 3 capacities $\times$ 8 ratios $\times$ 5 seeds on T1, with 20
visits per prototype and $\max(5,C/2)$ rare experiences; the substrate
benchmark, 4 seeds; CIFAR-100, 5 ratios $\times$ 3 seeds at $C=20$; MiniGrid,
40 rollouts per condition (200 in five seed blocks in the extended run). For
an axis, $\eta^2 = SS_{between}/SS_{total}$ over the 576 configuration scores:
a one-way, marginal decomposition. The factorial is full and balanced, so main
effects are orthogonal; two-way interactions are cell-mean residuals
($SS_{ab}/SS_{total}$), reported separately. Scores are deterministic given the
seed, so there is no error term and $\eta^2$ is descriptive. Bands are 95\,\%
percentile bootstraps (2000 resamples) with the seed as unit; a capacity's
crossover is the geometric mean of the last write-dominant and first
eviction-dominant $r$.

\emph{Structural dependence.} The axes are not functionally independent, and
the decomposition does not assume they are. Strength is consumed only by
\texttt{min\_strength}, so \texttt{strength} and \texttt{reinforce} are inert in
the three quarters of the space that never read it; \texttt{merge} acts only
above its threshold; and under a constant gate with \texttt{append} writes and
no reinforcement \texttt{min\_strength} reduces exactly to \texttt{fifo}. A
small main effect is therefore not evidence of a weak mechanism, and wherever a
dependence is known we report the conditional effect. The two-way shares locate
it: \texttt{strength}$\times$\texttt{evict} explains
\result{exp01_nas_full:variance_share.strength×evict|pct}{4.3}\,\% of the
variance and \texttt{write}$\times$\texttt{evict}
\result{exp01_nas_full:variance_share.write×evict|pct}{7.8}\,\%.

\section{The e-MDB proxy and the frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

\subsection{Where the proxy lands}

Every axis is observable: a liveness audit, run in continuous integration,
measures the largest score change between configurations differing only in
that axis, and all six clear it. Write mode is the strongest main effect
(\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.0.eta2|pct}{56.6}\,\%), followed by
eviction (\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.1.eta2|pct}{17.0}\,\%), decay
(\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.2.eta2|pct}{1.8}\,\%) and strength
(\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.3.eta2|pct}{1.4}\,\%); read and
reinforcement explain
\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.5.eta2|pct}{0.0}\,\% and
\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.4.eta2|pct}{0.0}\,\% as main effects
despite positive observability
(\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.5.liveness|f3}{0.119} and
\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.4.liveness|f3}{0.206}).

The FIFO-NN proxy scores \result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.score|f3}{0.106},
rank \result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.rank_optimistic|int}{416}--%
\result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.rank_pessimistic|int}{469} of 576 with
\result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.n_tied|int}{54} ties. It is not the floor:
the configurations below it all write by \texttt{append} and combine
minimum-utility eviction, \texttt{topk3} reads, or \texttt{min\_strength} with
reinforcement. The frontier scores
\result{exp01_nas_full:frontier.score|f3}{0.818}, almost eight times more.

\subsection{Two axes, neither alone}
\label{sec:interaction}

The frontier is \texttt{\{read: nn, write: append, strength: both, decay: 1.0,
evict: min\_strength, reinforce: 0\}}: every experience is stored as its own
trace with a weight proportional to its surprise, weights never decay or change
on recall, and when memory fills the least surprising trace is discarded. It
differs from the proxy on exactly two axes, and changing either alone leaves the
proxy bit-identical (Table~\ref{tab:two-knobs}); the salience gate alone is
inert in every combination of the other axes. The reason is exact: under a
constant gate with \texttt{append} and no reinforcement every trace has
strength $1.0$, so the minimum is always the oldest trace ---\texttt{min\_strength}
\emph{is} FIFO--- and a gate feeding a policy that never reads strength
computes a weight nothing consumes.

The same dependence explains why the gate's
\result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.3.eta2|pct}{1.4}\,\% main effect misleads.

\section{The regime transition}
\label{sec:regime}

The \result{exp01_nas_full:main_effects.0.eta2|pct}{56.6}\,\% of the write axis
is less a property of merging than of $r$. We sweep the space over capacities
and ratios, holding visits per prototype fixed so that $r$ is not confounded
with recurrence. The attribution switches abruptly from the write axis to
eviction near $r=1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold}): the crossover, averaged over
$C=10$, 20 and 40, is \result{exp02_threshold_grid:r_umbral_medio|f2}{0.88}
(descriptive interval
[\result{exp02_threshold_grid:r_umbral_ci.0|f2}{0.87},
\result{exp02_threshold_grid:r_umbral_ci.1|f2}{0.89}]), and the three
capacities fall in the same grid interval. The mechanism is close to a
restatement of what capacity means: eviction runs only when a write would
exceed capacity, so below $r=1$ recurring experience fits, merging compresses
it and frees slots for the rare (the \emph{compression} regime), while above it
memory is saturated and the question becomes which policy preserves what
matters (\emph{selection}). What the grid adds is that the switch is abrupt,
not that it exists. $\eta^2$ is direction-blind: it shows that the write axis
moves the outcome, not that merging is preferable, and above the threshold
write and eviction jointly explain only about a fifth of the variance, the rest
belonging to the gate, decay and their interaction with eviction.


\emph{Nominal or effective ratio.} Repeated on 23 stream families
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), nominal $r$ is not the governing variable: its
crossover ranges from
\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_nom.cruce_min|f2}{0.16} to
\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_nom.cruce_max|f2}{1.32} (s.d.\
of its log \result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_nom.sd_log|f2}{0.46});
drift and noise pull it far below 1, at the noisiest setting eviction
dominates at every $r$, and even capacity moves it, from
\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_nom.cruces.C005|f2}{0.71} at
$C=5$ to \result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_nom.cruces.C160|f2}{0.98}
at $C=160$. Over $r_{eff}=K_{effective}/C$, where $K_{effective}$ counts the
traces the routine occupies after consolidation, crossovers collapse to
[\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_eff.cruce_min|f2}{0.56},
\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_eff.cruce_max|f2}{1.28}] (s.d.\
\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_eff.sd_log|f2}{0.17}), tighter
than nominal $r$ in
\result{exp10_law_robustness:parte_a.colapso.r_eff.frac_bootstrap_mas_colapsada|pct}{99.8}\,\%
of bootstrap replicates and than four other pressure measures fixed in advance;
rare-event prevalence and $C=5$ leave the residual spread. The hypothesis is
therefore stated over $r_{eff}$, which equals $r$ when encounters consolidate
cleanly, as in Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold}.

\emph{Merge threshold.} Sweeping it from 0.30 to 0.97 ($C=20$, five seeds)
leaves the nominal crossover at
$r\approx\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.synthetic.umbrales.t085.cruce_r_nominal|f2}{0.87}$
for every threshold from 0.40 to 0.85; at 0.90 merging misses part of the visits
and it drops to
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.synthetic.umbrales.t090.cruce_r_nominal|f2}{0.61};
at 0.30 merging absorbs
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.synthetic.umbrales.t030.raros_absorbidos_r_min|pct}{38.0}\,\%
of rare events; from 0.95 merging fragments prototypes and eviction dominates at
every $r$. Over $K_{effective}$ the crossover stays between
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.synthetic.umbrales.t040.cruce_r_efectivo|f2}{0.86}
and \result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.synthetic.umbrales.t030.cruce_r_efectivo|f2}{1.02}
for every threshold from 0.30 to 0.90, and a sweep of the gate's gain
(0.5--8) leaves the pattern unchanged.

\section{Real data}

\subsection{CIFAR-100: the separability precondition}

The synthetic keys are nearly orthogonal Gaussian vectors, which could
overstate retrieval. We rerun the grid on CIFAR-100 \cite{krizhevsky2009}
embeddings from a pretrained ResNet-18 \cite{he2016}, reduced by principal
component analysis to 32 dimensions. With this extractor the write axis is
inoperative in all five cells, explaining
\result{exp05_real_embeddings:dominios.cifar100.celdas.4.eta2_write|pct}{0.0}--\result{exp05_real_embeddings:dominios.cifar100.celdas.1.eta2_write|pct}{0.4}\,\%
of the variance whatever the ratio, while the synthetic domain in the same run
crosses normally
($\hat r \approx \result{exp05_real_embeddings:dominios.synthetic.r_umbral|f2}{0.77}$
over the estimated $\hat K/C$). The deciding quantity is \emph{intra-prototype
similarity}, how alike two encounters with one class look. Its median,
\result{exp05_real_embeddings:precondicion_cifar100.similitud_intra_prototipo|f3}{0.401},
is far below the 0.85 threshold, so merging almost never fires and
$K_{effective}$ grows from
\result{exp05_real_embeddings:dominios.cifar100.celdas.0.k_efectivo|int}{84} to
\result{exp05_real_embeddings:dominios.cifar100.celdas.4.k_efectivo|int}{1435}
traces as nominal $K$ rises from 5 to 80 at $C=20$, instead of converging to
$K_{proto}$. Lowering the threshold does not help, because intra-class
similarity (median
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.cifar100.similitudes.intra.p50|f2}{0.38})
overlaps inter-class similarity (95th percentile
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.cifar100.similitudes.inter.p95|f2}{0.42}):
no threshold from 0.30 to 0.97 yields merges both frequent and pure, and from
0.40 up eviction dominates on average. At 0.30 the write axis regains dominance
at low $r$, but not as useful compression: only
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.cifar100.umbrales.t030.pureza_fusion_r_min|pct}{47.7}\,\%
of merges join the same class,
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.cifar100.umbrales.t030.raros_absorbidos_r_min|pct}{70.0}\,\%
of rare events are absorbed on arrival, and the best merging configuration
retains
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.cifar100.umbrales.t030.celdas.r0p25.puntaje_mejor_merge.media|f2}{0.30}
against
\result{exp07_merge_sensitivity:umbral_fusion.cifar100.umbrales.t030.celdas.r0p25.puntaje_frontera.media|f2}{1.00}
for the \texttt{append} frontier.

The precondition is thus \emph{separability} ---a threshold that repeated
encounters clear and distinct ones do not--- and it is not a term in $r$ but a
check that comes first: where consolidation is partial, sub-threshold visits
fragment a prototype and the hypothesis holds over $r_{eff}$; where it is
unreachable, $K_{effective}$ grows with the stream and no ratio, nominal or
effective, produces a compression regime. Estimating $K_{proto}$ does not
rescue it either. We take the cluster count maximizing the mean silhouette
coefficient, require a silhouette of at least $0.15$ before treating $r$ as
defined (clustering returns an argmax even on isotropic noise), and report a
bootstrap interval; on CIFAR-100 the intervals reach the ceiling of the search
in several cells, consistent with no compact cluster structure.

\subsection{MiniGrid: aligning surprise with importance}
\label{sec:minigrid}

A robot does not receive a stream: it receives the consequences of its own
actions, under partial observation. On \texttt{MiniGrid-MemoryS13-v0}
\cite{minigrid}, where the agent must remember an object seen at the start to
choose correctly at the end, we compare the frontier and the FIFO on rare-event
retention, with rewarded steps as the important events (40 rollouts per
condition). Under a random policy, with surprise from the one-step prediction
error of an online linear transition model, both sit at the floor
(\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_aleatoria_prediccion.resultados.frontera.tasa|pct}{0.0}\,\%
and
\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_aleatoria_prediccion.resultados.FIFO.tasa|pct}{2.6}\,\%):
\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_aleatoria_prediccion.separacion_de_saliencia.fraccion_comunes_sobre_minimo_raro|pct}{75.4}\,\%
of routine steps are at least as surprising as the least surprising of the
\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_aleatoria_prediccion.separacion_de_saliencia.n_raros|int}{38}
rewarded ones. A forward-biased policy quadruples the rewarded events
(\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_sesgada_prediccion.n_raros_total|int}{156})
without changing this overlap
(\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_sesgada_prediccion.separacion_de_saliencia.fraccion_comunes_sobre_minimo_raro|pct}{73.5}\,\%)
or the floor, so the problem is not sample size. The dopaminergic account that
motivates the gate concerns \emph{reward}-prediction error
\cite{schultz1997,rouhani2018}. Predicting reward instead, with the same model
and policy, cuts the overlap to
\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_aleatoria_recompensa.separacion_de_saliencia.fraccion_comunes_sobre_minimo_raro|pct}{5.6}\,\%
and lifts the frontier to
\result{exp06_minigrid:comparacion_aleatoria_recompensa.resultados.frontera.tasa|pct}{52.6}\,\%

[\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:exp06_original.aleatoria_recompensa.resultados.frontera.cluster_low|pct}{37.9},

while the FIFO, which never reads strength, stays at

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{What holds against e-MDB.} On the synthetic battery, with a separable
signal, the frontier outscores the FIFO-NN proxy by a factor of almost eight
and retains nearly every rare event where the proxy keeps one in twenty; the
lead over the proxy survives 13 of 14 held-out stream families and, at
$r=0.25$, every surprise degradation we tried; no external rule beats the
frontier; and the buffer's real sequential read, under our assumed match rule,
scores lower still. In MiniGrid,
with a signal aligned with importance, it retains about half of the important
events against almost none. This is not a criticism of e-MDB, whose buffer
feeds model training rather than recall, but a specific claim: as a store of
what to remember, the buffer as implemented is the floor at rare-event
retention under capacity pressure. Given a surprise signal that separates what
matters, the recommendation does not require a robot to know its own
$r_{eff}$, since the frontier never merges.

\emph{What does not.} Four results bound that claim. First, \emph{retaining
better is not yet acting better}. An episodic controller \cite{pritzel2017}
whose only experience is the memory faced four MiniGrid goal variants that
alternate and recur (ten seeds). At $C=300$ the frontier earns more than FIFO
on the first episodes of a returning task
($+\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.diferencias_pareadas.frontera_menos_FIFO.reaparicion.media|f3}{0.038}$,
95\,\% CI
$[\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.diferencias_pareadas.frontera_menos_FIFO.reaparicion.ci_low|f3}{0.005},
\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.diferencias_pareadas.frontera_menos_FIFO.reaparicion.ci_high|f3}{0.071}]$)
but integrates less return over the stream (area under the curve
$\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.diferencias_pareadas.frontera_menos_FIFO.auc.media|f3}{-0.075}$,
$[\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.diferencias_pareadas.frontera_menos_FIFO.auc.ci_low|f3}{-0.098},
\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.diferencias_pareadas.frontera_menos_FIFO.auc.ci_high|f3}{-0.053}]$,
no seed favouring it), and likewise at $C=1000$: without decay, retention costs
plasticity. Decay (0.995 or 0.98) lifts its area to the FIFO level, and its
return-task advantage shrinks to within noise of FIFO (lost at $C=1000$): no
tested configuration is significantly better than FIFO on both. The frontier
does beat reservoir sampling on return, and an unbounded memory
(\result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.sin_limite.auc.media|f3}{0.566}
against \result{exp13_downstream_learning:resumen.C300.frontera.auc.media|f3}{0.144})
shows how far every bounded policy remains from the ceiling. Second, \emph{at
equal bytes the FIFO wins}: at SDM's budget (144\,KiB) a FIFO holds
\result{exp12_substrate_cost:igual_presupuesto.defaults.B147456.filas.FIFO.capacity|int}{921}
traces, more than T1 writes, so no capacity pressure is left, and scores
\result{exp12_substrate_cost:igual_presupuesto.defaults.B147456.filas.FIFO.battery_mean|f3}{0.924}
against SDM's
\result{exp12_substrate_cost:igual_presupuesto.defaults.B147456.filas.SDM.battery_mean|f3}{0.745}:
SDM's footprint buys nothing a FIFO of equal bytes cannot match, and the
advantage measured here is one of selection under capacity pressure. Third, \emph{the advantage depends on the quality of the
surprise signal}: the factor of
\result{exp01_nas_full:salience_conditional_ratio|f2}{14.41} belongs to a
separable signal, and at $r=0.25$ a surprise AUC below about
\result{exp08_imperfect_salience:settings.main.conditions.auc950.auc.mean|f2}{0.95}
hands the advantage to a configuration that merges. In MiniGrid perceptual prediction error leaves retention at the
floor, and when importance is delayed no causal signal reliably retains the
steps that precede a reward: reward-prediction error beats the null in
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.previa_k.reward_pe.gana_vs_azar|int}{0}
of \result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.previa_k.reward_pe.n_celdas|int}{15}
cells, TD error in
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.previa_k.td_error.gana_vs_azar|int}{2}
and count novelty in
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.previa_k.count_novelty.gana_vs_azar|int}{6},
with a mean gain of
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.previa_k.count_novelty.ganancia_vs_azar_media|pct}{1.8}
points, while only the non-causal discounted return does
(\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.previa_k.retorno.gana_vs_azar|int}{15});
when importance is a key event, not even the return beats the null
(\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.evento_clave.retorno.gana_vs_azar|int}{0}
of \result{exp11_minigrid_extended:resumen.evento_clave.retorno.n_celdas|int}{8}).
Fourth, \emph{the regime is set by $r_{eff}$, not nominal $r$}: drift or noisy
encounters fragment one recurring situation into several traces and push a
robot into selection earlier than counting kinds of situation suggests.

\emph{The open tension.} LOLA is defined by the absence of predefined goals,
and an open-ended robot may face long stretches with no reward to predict; the
available signal then falls back to perceptual prediction error, where we
measured retention at the floor. The fix is valid where a reward exists and
unproven where it does not, the regime e-MDB is built for. Learning progress or
the needs and drives of e-MDB's motivational engine \cite{becerra2021} are
testable substitutes that we do not evaluate.

\emph{Untested.} EMBER is not integrated into the running e-MDB:
Fig.~\ref{fig:integration} is a design, the buffer's read was re-implemented
rather than run, and whether better retention improves the world and utility
models e-MDB trains from its buffer is untested. The separability check was
measured with class labels; a label-free online estimate is untested. The
real-data evidence uses one visual extractor (ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18,
PCA to 32 dimensions), one dataset and one capacity, so whether the low
intra-class similarity belongs to the domain or the extractor is open; a
fine-tuned extractor could change it, a lower merge threshold does not. MiniGrid
spans four environments and four policies but stays within small discrete grid
worlds, synthetic drift enters only the robustness families, and no experiment
ran on a physical robot, so real sensor noise, continuous control and lifelong
non-stationarity remain untested.

\section{Conclusion}

Treating the e-MDB episodic buffer as one point of an exhaustively enumerated
design space shows what it lacks and what fixes it. Two axes separate it from
the frontier ---surprise-weighted encoding and weakest-trace eviction--- and,
starting from the buffer's proxy, neither does anything without the other.
Together they lift the aggregate score
from \result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.score|f3}{0.106} to
\result{exp01_nas_full:frontier.score|f3}{0.818} and rare-event retention from
\result{exp01_nas_full:incumbent.scores.rare_retention|f3}{0.050} to
\result{exp01_nas_full:frontier.scores.rare_retention|f3}{0.983} on a separable
signal; the lead over the proxy holds on held-out stream families and, when
recurring experience fits in memory, even at chance-level surprise; no external
rule beats it; the buffer's real read scores
lower still, and in MiniGrid the frontier retains
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:exp06_ampliado.aleatoria_recompensa.resultados.frontera.tasa|pct}{47.2}\,\%
of rewarded events against
\result{exp11_minigrid_extended:exp06_ampliado.aleatoria_recompensa.resultados.FIFO.tasa|pct}{0.7}\,\%.
Which mechanism matters is set by the regime, located by $r_{eff}$; SDM's
retention comes from gated eviction with exact erasure; a pure spiking circuit
needs an encoding layer for continuous vectors; and consolidation and salience
each carry a precondition ---separable encounters, and a surprise signal
aligned with importance--- that only real data reveal. The claim is about
retention under capacity pressure: whether it becomes better behaviour, and
how to define salience without reward, are the questions that remain.
